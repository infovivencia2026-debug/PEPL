## 1. Executive summary

PEPL's foundations are strong: every tenant table has FORCE RLS with a `current_tenant()` policy, tenant context is transaction-local, definer functions pin `search_path`, tokens are 256-bit and hashed, money is integer paise, locked payroll is trigger-protected, and the operator/tenant identity split is well built. The review found **no cross-tenant read** through the normal request path.

The risk sits in the seams between the strong parts:

1. **The two routers disagree.** The browser (`/api/ui`) and the API (`/api/v1`) implement the same actions differently. Payroll calculation in the browser computes **no TDS** (confirmed); PT month rules, approval guards, and login normalisation differ between them.
2. **Abuse controls have holes.** Rate limiting is bypassed by any junk `Bearer` header (confirmed), which also makes tenant-MFA brute force and login spraying practical; tenant MFA has no lockout at all.
2. **"Immutable / append-only" is weaker than documented.** Default privileges give the app role UPDATE/DELETE almost everywhere; several "never deleted" tables can be deleted, a global statutory table is writable, and the payroll lock trigger can be sidestepped by moving a row's `run_id` (confirmed).
4. **Lifecycle gaps.** Terminated employees keep working sessions; suspended customers are silently re-activated by the dunning job; revision payroll runs re-pay the full net.
5. **Gates and tests prove less than they claim.** `gate:rls` accepts `USING (true) WITH CHECK (tenant_id = current_tenant())`; 433 of 539 routes have no HTTP test; the docs describe controls (argon2id, refresh tokens, column encryption, Redis, golden-file payroll gate) that do not exist.
